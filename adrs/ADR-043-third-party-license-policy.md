# ADR-043: License Policy for Third-Party Components

**Status**: accepted
**Date**: 2026-10-15
**Deciders**: [Lukas Mathis – Repository Owner](../MAINTAINERS.md)
**Consulted**: –
**Informed**: all contributors

## Context and Problem

OEA is dual-licensed (see [NOTICE](../NOTICE)): the Community Edition under AGPL-3.0-or-later and the Enterprise Edition under a proprietary license ([LICENSES/LICENSE-ENTERPRISE.md](../LICENSES/LICENSE-ENTERPRISE.md)). The licensor grants rights only in OEA's own code; every third-party component that OEA ships stays under its own license, which reaches every recipient directly.

So far no rule decides which licenses third-party components may have, and no document lists the components and their licenses. The owner asked for a third-party license list and for the rule that no component is under a stricter license than OEA's.

"Not stricter than AGPL-3.0" alone is not sufficient: the GPL is compatible with the AGPL, but a GPL library linked into OEA would make the proprietary Enterprise license impossible. ADR-040 already rejected a component (PrimeVue 5) on license grounds, case by case.

Inventory on 2026-10-15:

- Backend jar: 115 runtime libraries, almost all under Apache-2.0, MIT or BSD licenses; Eclipse licenses (EPL-2.0, EDL-1.0) for the Jakarta APIs, JAXB, AspectJ and Logback; Logback and two Jakarta APIs are additionally offered under LGPL-2.1 and GPL-2.0 with Classpath Exception
- Frontend bundle: 19 packages, all under MIT, ISC or Apache-2.0
- Container images: Eclipse Temurin JRE (OpenJDK, GPL-2.0 with Classpath Exception) on Ubuntu, PostgreSQL (PostgreSQL License) on Alpine

## Decision Drivers

- Both editions must remain possible: the Enterprise Edition needs third-party licenses that allow proprietary distribution
- Recipients of either edition obtain the rights in third-party components directly from their licenses, without separate agreements
- Obligations of the distributor (license texts, notices, source of weak-copyleft components) must be met with every release
- The rule must be enforced by CI, not by review alone, and must not block on tools that are never distributed
- Little maintenance: generated from the build, no hand-written list

## Considered Options

### Option 1: Permissive and weak-copyleft licenses for distributed components

Allowed for components distributed with OEA (backend runtime libraries, frontend bundle): permissive licenses (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD-2-Clause, BSD-3-Clause, ISC, 0BSD, CC0-1.0, Unlicense, BlueOak-1.0.0, Bouncy Castle License, PostgreSQL License) and weak-copyleft licenses for unmodified libraries (EPL-1.0, EPL-2.0, MPL-2.0, LGPL-2.1, LGPL-3.0). Forbidden: strong copyleft (GPL, AGPL), source-available licenses (SSPL, BUSL), proprietary licenses and components without license. For a dual-licensed component one allowed alternative suffices.

- **Pro**:
  - Compatible with AGPL-3.0 and with proprietary distribution of the Enterprise Edition
  - Matches the current inventory without exceptions
  - Weak copyleft obligations are limited to the unmodified library files (source availability, license text)
- **Con**:
  - Weak-copyleft components require stating where their source is available; changes to them would have to be published

### Option 2: Permissive licenses only

- **Pro**:
  - Simplest obligations
- **Con**:
  - Excludes the Jakarta APIs, JAXB, AspectJ and Logback (EPL-2.0/EDL), which Spring Boot and Hibernate require; the stack would need exceptions from the start

### Option 3: Every license compatible with AGPL-3.0

- **Pro**:
  - Literal reading of "not stricter than the AGPL"
- **Con**:
  - Allows GPL libraries, which would rule out the proprietary Enterprise Edition

## Decision

We choose **Option 1**.

1. **Scope**: components distributed with OEA – the runtime libraries in the backend jar and the packages in the frontend bundle. Build, test and development tools are not distributed and not covered; container base images are listed as runtime environment (point 4).
2. **Allowed and forbidden licenses** as in Option 1; the list of allowed SPDX identifiers lives in `scripts/generate_third_party_licenses.py`. Adding a license to it needs an amendment of this ADR.
3. **Third-party list**: `THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.md` in the repository root is generated by `scripts/generate_third_party_licenses.py` from the Maven license report of the backend and the package manifest of the frontend build. The frontend build also ships the license texts of the bundled packages (`third-party-licenses.txt`); the backend jar contains the license and notice files of its libraries.
4. **Runtime environment**: the base images of the shipped container images are listed with their main licenses. OEA does not link against the operating system; the JRE's GPL-2.0 is covered by the Classpath Exception. They are not subject to point 2.
5. **Enforcement**: a CI job regenerates the list and fails if a component has a forbidden or unknown license or if `THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.md` is outdated.
6. **Licenses of OEA**: neither the AGPL-3.0 nor the Enterprise license extends to third-party components; both say so ([NOTICE](../NOTICE), [LICENSES/LICENSE-ENTERPRISE.md](../LICENSES/LICENSE-ENTERPRISE.md)).

## Consequences

### Positive Consequences

- Every release carries a complete, current list of third-party components and licenses
- A dependency under a forbidden license fails CI before it reaches `main`
- Enterprise customers need no separate agreements with third-party licensors

### Negative Consequences / Trade-offs

- A new dependency that brings a new allowed license needs a change of the script (and, for a new license category, of this ADR)
- The license report depends on the license metadata of the published artifacts; missing or wrong metadata has to be corrected by mapping entries in the script

### Follow-up Decisions

- Legal review of the license wording of the Enterprise Edition (open item, together with the CLA)

Owner decision of 2026-10-15: "I would like a third-party license list that shows which libraries the tool needs and under which license they are. None should be stricter than my license." The owner accepted the stricter reading of Option 1 because of the Enterprise Edition.

The license texts of OEA are not legal advice; the wording should be reviewed by a lawyer specialized in open-source licensing.

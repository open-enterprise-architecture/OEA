# Diagrams

A diagram models a business process or another view of the architecture with the notation of a [viewpoint](../administration/metamodel-editing#viewpoints), for example *Process Model (BPMN 2.0)*. You create, find, rename, delete and restore diagrams as objects, and you place the elements of the viewpoint on the canvas of a diagram.

You need the permission `entity:read` (for example the role `viewer`) to see diagrams. Creating, renaming and deleting needs `entity:write`, restoring `entity:restore` (role `enterprise-architect`). Service accounts with `entity:write` can create and change diagrams through the API like persons.

## Find diagrams

You find diagrams in three places:

- **The page *Diagrams*:** choose *Diagrams* in the header. The page lists the diagrams by name, regardless of upper and lower case, each with its viewpoint, the notation of the viewpoint and the time of the last change. Type a part of a name into *Filter by name* to narrow the list; characters such as `%` and `_` are matched as typed. The list shows 50 diagrams at a time; choose *Load more* for the next ones. Without any diagram the page says *No diagrams yet.*
- **The browser:** the category *Diagrams* of *Current state* in the sidebar lists the diagrams with the notation as a small label (see [Navigation tree](./navigation#browser)).
- **The navigation tree:** diagrams that were placed in a folder (see [Place a diagram](./navigation#place-a-diagram)).

Choose a diagram to open its page.

## Create a diagram

1. Choose *Create diagram* on the page *Diagrams*.
2. Fill in the dialog:

   | Field | Meaning |
   |---|---|
   | Name | required, 1 to 200 characters; unique among the diagrams, regardless of upper and lower case; surrounding blanks are removed |
   | Description | optional, up to 2000 characters |
   | Viewpoint | required; the viewpoint of the diagram and its notation |

3. Choose *Create*. The new, empty diagram opens.

You can change the viewpoint later (see [Change the viewpoint](#change-the-viewpoint)). If the name is taken, the dialog says *A diagram with this name already exists.*

## The canvas

The page of a diagram shows its name, description and viewpoint, and below them the canvas with the shapes of the diagram. Each shape is a box with the name of the entity, the abbreviation of its type and, if the viewpoint gives one, a border in the color of its type. **A shape is a view of an entity: the entity itself stays in the repository.** The same entity may appear several times in one diagram. A diagram shows only entities of the types its viewpoint allows. Connections are drawn as edges between the shapes (see [Connections](#connections)). In a diagram of a viewpoint with the notation BPMN 2.0 the shapes and edges are drawn with their BPMN symbols (see [BPMN process diagrams](#bpmn)); in the other notations every shape is a box.

Pan the canvas by dragging the background and zoom with the mouse wheel or with the buttons *Zoom in*, *Zoom out* and *Fit view* above the canvas. With the permission `entity:read` you can look at the diagram this way; the canvas is then marked *Read-only* and has no palette, no *Add existing* and no *Save*.

With the permission `entity:write` you also edit the layout:

- **Place a new entity:** the palette above the canvas lists the entity types the viewpoint allows. Click a type to place a new shape at the center of the visible canvas, or drag the type onto the canvas. Type the name into the new shape and press Enter. Without a name the shape disappears again (also with Escape). The shape gets the default size of its type in the viewpoint, in points (pt); without a stored size that is 90 × 30 pt.
- **Place an existing entity:** *Add existing* opens a search. Type a part of the name (regardless of upper and lower case; characters such as `%` and `_` are matched as typed) and choose the entity; *Load more* shows further matches. The search finds only entities of the types the viewpoint allows. The new shape appears at the center of the visible canvas.
- **Move and resize:** drag a shape, and drag the handles of a selected shape to resize it. With the keyboard, select a shape (Tab or click) and move it with the arrow keys by 1 pt, or by 10 pt with Shift. Positions are within ±100 000 pt, sizes between 1 and 10 000 pt, with two decimals at most. A shape that lies in a container follows it when the container is moved (see [Nested shapes](#nested-shapes)).
- **Remove from diagram:** select a shape and choose *Remove from diagram* above the canvas, press Delete, or open the context menu of the shape with the right mouse button. The shape disappears from the diagram; **the entity stays in the repository.**
- **Delete from repository:** with `entity:delete`, choose *Delete from repository* in the context menu of a shape or an edge, or press Ctrl+Delete on the selected one. The dialog names what the deletion takes along: the children of the entity on all levels, the connections of the entity and its children, and the active solutions that have changes to them. After you confirm, the entity and everything deleted with it are deleted at once, **without the explicit save**, and their shapes and edges disappear from the canvas; your other unsaved changes stay. Other diagrams hide the shapes while the entity is deleted and show them again after it was restored (needs `entity:restore`; see [Deleted entities](./deleted-entities)). If someone changed the entity after you opened the dialog, OEA says so and offers *Read again*. An entity with open issues of a solution cannot be deleted; a structuring aid and an automatic composition connection are not deleted this way. Changes of active solutions to a deleted entity become [conflicts](./solutions#go-live) that the solution resolves before its go-live. The external ID of a deleted entity stays reserved.
- **Details of an entity:** select a shape to open its details next to the canvas (see [Details of an entity](#details-of-an-entity)).
- **Save:** *Save* stores all changes in one step, including the changes of entities made in the details. Until then, the canvas shows *Unsaved changes*, and new or changed shapes carry a small dot. A diagram holds at most 2000 shapes, and one save creates at most 500 new entities; the canvas tells when a limit is reached. Edges are saved in the same step (see [Connections](#connections)).

If you leave the page with unsaved changes, OEA asks whether to discard them. If someone else (or you in another window) changed the diagram since you opened it, *Save* answers *The diagram was changed by someone else*; choose *Reload and discard my changes* to continue with the current state, or *Keep editing* to copy your work elsewhere first. Renaming the diagram counts as a change too.

If a shape cannot be saved, for example because its entity was deleted meanwhile, the canvas marks the shape with the reason and keeps all your changes; nothing is saved until every shape is valid. A shape whose type the viewpoint does not allow is a *conflict* and has its own rules (see [Change the viewpoint](#change-the-viewpoint)).

### Entities with missing values

A new entity created on the canvas has a name and a type only. If its type has a mandatory property without a default value, the entity is still created, and it counts as **incomplete** until the value is written. Every shape of an incomplete entity shows *Incomplete:* with the names of the missing properties. Other changes of the entity, for example through the batch API, still require the mandatory values. You complete the entity in its details (see below), or write the values through the API (see the [API reference](../api/)).

If an entity is deleted, its shapes are hidden from the diagram; they show again when the entity is restored.

## Nested shapes {#nested-shapes}

The hierarchy of the entities (see [Arrange components](./navigation#arrange-components)) can be shown on the canvas: a shape may lie in another shape of the same diagram, its **container**, like a composition in ArchiMate. A container is a shape that holds other shapes; it carries its name above them and grows when you move a shape beyond its edge. It never shrinks below the shapes it holds, and a shape lies completely within its container, at most 10 levels deep. **Nesting alone changes nothing in the repository**; whether the parent of an entity changes is asked separately (see below).

- **Show the children:** when you place an entity that has children with *Add existing*, OEA asks *Show children?*. With *Show children* the direct children whose type the viewpoint allows and that the diagram does not show yet are nested in a grid in the new shape, and the shape grows to hold them. A notice names the children of a type the viewpoint does not allow, which are not placed, and says when only the first 500 children were considered. With *Only this entity*, or when you close the dialog, only the entity is placed. Only the direct children are shown, not their children; a child that has children offers *Show children* itself.
- **Show them later:** *Show children* in the context menu of a shape does the same for a shape that is placed already; the children that the diagram shows already are not placed again.
- **A child that is shown elsewhere:** it is not nested a second time. If its automatic composition connection to the parent is allowed in the viewpoint, that connection is drawn as an edge. Otherwise a **hierarchy line** runs from the container to the shape of the child: a dashed line without an arrowhead. Select it with Tab or a click and remove it with *Remove from diagram* or Delete; the hierarchy stays as it is. The line is shown only while the entity of the child is a child of the entity of the container.
- **Move a shape into a container or out of it:** drag it so that its center lies in the other shape, or out of its container. The shape is nested, the container grows to hold it, and if the setting asks (see below) and the entity has other parents, OEA asks *New parent?*. *Yes* changes the parent of the entity with the next save, with the rules of [arranging components](./navigation#arrange-components): a cycle or more than 10 levels are rejected, an entity with open issues of a solution cannot get a new parent, and the entity gets a new version. Leaving a container asks whether the entity has no parent anymore. *No* changes only the picture. OEA asks nothing when the entity has that parent already, when you move a shape within its container, and when the setting does not ask.
- **A new element in a container:** a new element that you drag from the palette onto a container is nested in it and created **as a child of the entity of the container**, without a question. Dropped onto a container that is new, it becomes a child of the new entity; both are created with one save.
- **Remove a container:** *Remove from diagram* on a shape that holds other shapes asks for a confirmation that names the number of nested shapes: they leave the diagram with the container, and their edges and lines with them. **The entities stay in the repository.**

The setting that decides whether OEA asks *New parent?* is configured for the whole instance and can be overridden for each viewpoint (see [Viewpoints](../administration/metamodel-editing#viewpoints)). If someone else changed an entity whose parent you changed, *Save* names it and offers *Reload*, as for other changes of entities; reloading discards your change of its parent. A container whose entity was deleted is hidden; the shapes nested in it stay at their positions and show without a container. If the container returns, the shapes that were saved meanwhile do not return into it.

## BPMN process diagrams {#bpmn}

A diagram of the viewpoint *Process Model (BPMN 2.0)* models a business process with the types of the starter package *BPMN 2.0 process modeling* (see [Metamodel packages](../administration/metamodel-packages#bpmn-package)). The viewpoint comes with the package; import the package first if your instance has no such viewpoint.

- **Symbols:** the canvas draws each shape with the symbol of its type, not as a box. A task is a rounded rectangle with the name inside and a marker at the top left (person for a user task, gear for a service task, filled envelope for a send task, open envelope for a receive task, table for a business rule task, page for a script task). A call activity has a thick border and a sub-process shows its name at the top. Events are circles with the name below: a thin line for a start event, a thick one for an end event, a double one for an intermediate event, with the markers envelope (message), clock (timer) and filled circle (terminate). A gateway is a diamond with the name below and the marker X (exclusive), + (parallel), O (inclusive) or a pentagon in a circle (event-based). A pool and a lane are rectangles with the name in a band on the left. A text annotation is an open bracket that shows the description of the entity, or the name if it has none. A group is a dashed rounded rectangle without fill. A type that has no BPMN symbol in the viewpoint, for example *Process*, stays a box.
- **Flows:** a *Sequence Flow* is a solid line with a filled arrow, a *Message Flow* a dashed line with an open circle at the start and an open arrow at the end, and an *Association* a dotted line without an arrow. A sequence flow or message flow runs only between the BPMN elements that its type allows; to or from another type (for example an *Application Component*) the type is not offered, and the API refuses the flow with `422`.
- **Palette:** the palette is grouped into *Pools and lanes*, *Activities*, *Events*, *Gateways*, *Artifacts* and *Other* (types without a BPMN symbol). A subtype that your organization created, for example *Approval Task* of *User Task*, appears under the group of its symbol once a viewpoint allows it.
- **Pools, lanes and sub-processes hold other shapes:** only these three take nested shapes. A new element dropped onto a task, a group or a text annotation is not nested in it but in the pool, lane or sub-process below it, if there is one. Moving a shape works as described under [Nested shapes](#nested-shapes), including *New parent?*, which names the lane or pool. *Show children* is offered only for a pool, a lane and a sub-process.
- **Warnings of the pool rules:** a sequence flow whose ends lie in different pools, or one in a pool and one in none, is marked with a warning and the text *Sequence flow crosses a pool boundary*. A message flow between two shapes of the same pool is marked *Message flow within one pool*. The toolbar shows *N BPMN warnings*; choosing it selects the first marked flow. **A warning never blocks *Save*:** the diagram is saved as it is, and the warning disappears as soon as you move a shape so that the rule holds. Warnings are computed from the layout; they are not stored.

- **Lanes stack automatically:** the lanes of a pool, and the lanes nested in a lane, fill the width of their container right of the label band and lie one below the other without gaps, in the order from top to bottom. A new lane dropped on the pool takes the pool's whole height when it is the first lane and its default height otherwise; the pool grows with each lane. Select a lane and drag its **lower edge** to change its height: the lanes below move down with their content and the pool follows. A lane is at least 30 pt high and at least as high as its content. Drag the left or right edge of a pool to make every lane wider or narrower; the content keeps its place.
- **Insert and reorder lanes:** a selected lane shows two small icons at its edges, *Insert lane above* and *Insert lane below* (for a vertical pool *Insert lane left* and *Insert lane right*). Choosing one adds a new lane of the same type next to it, as a child of the same pool or lane, and you enter its name. You can also drop a lane from the palette or drag an existing lane: dropped on the upper or lower half of another lane (left or right half in a vertical pool) it becomes its neighbour, dropped on the label band of a pool or lane it is nested there. Reordering lanes of the same pool does not ask *New parent?*; moving a lane into another pool does. A task that you drop on the label band of a pool with lanes belongs to the pool itself and keeps its place; the last lane grows to hold it.
- **Vertical pools:** open the context menu of a pool and choose *Vertical* (or *Horizontal* to switch back). In a vertical pool the label band lies at the top and the lanes lie side by side from the left, as wide as you make them with their **right edge**. Switching keeps the order of the lanes, the size of each lane across the flow and the place of the content within its lane. The setting is stored per diagram and applies to the pool and its lanes.
- **Collapse sub-processes:** a sub-process shows a small *−* button at the bottom. Choose it, or *Collapse* in the context menu, to draw the sub-process as a small box of 90 × 60 pt with a *+* marker; the shapes inside it, and the flows and lines that touch them, are hidden but stay in the diagram and in the repository. *+* or *Expand* shows everything again. The state is stored per diagram and saved with the layout, so the same sub-process can be collapsed in one diagram and open in another. Collapsing changes only the drawing: the sub-process keeps its full size for its lane and pool. Selecting a hidden shape, for example from the list of conflicts, expands the sub-process around it. Readers see the collapsed state but have no button.

## Assign lanes, pools and tasks {#assignments}

In a diagram of the viewpoint *Process Model (BPMN 2.0)* you show who performs and who is responsible: a lane or pool belongs to an **organizational unit** and performs **roles**, a task needs a **role**, and a user task has a **responsible person**. The types come with the starter package *Organization model* (see [Metamodel packages](../administration/metamodel-packages#organization-package)); the parts are chosen under [Organization model](../administration/metamodel-editing#organization-model). Without a part – for example without a person – the canvas offers nothing for it.

- **Lane:** open the context menu and choose *Assign organizational unit* or *Assign role*. A search lists the active entities of that type by name; choose one. The lane shows the unit as a badge and its roles as chips under its name (three chips, then *+N*). A lane has **one** organizational unit at most; assigning another one replaces the first. Several roles are fine.
- **Pool:** choose *Set participant* in the context menu; the pool header shows the unit.
- **Task:** the details panel has the field *Required role*, and for a user task also *Responsible person*; choose *Add*. The task shows the first responsible person as an avatar with the initials; the full name is its tooltip. A second person is accepted with the warning *More than one responsible person*.
- **Create new:** if the search has no hit, *Create new* creates an organizational unit, role or person with the name you typed, with the next *Save*; it has no shape in the diagram. For a type with subtypes you choose the type first.
- **Remove:** *×* on a badge, a chip or a person removes the assignment with the next *Save*. Removing a **saved** assignment deletes its connection softly and needs `entity:delete`; without it the entry and *×* are not offered. A change that you did not save yet you can always take back.
- **Save:** an assignment is a connection without an edge. It counts as an unsaved change, is saved with the layout in one step and audited like every connection (`entity.created`, and `entity.deleted` when removed). A save creates at most 500 connections and removes at most 500.

**Lanes of organizational units and roles assign instead of nesting:** if the viewpoint draws an organizational unit or a role as a lane or pool (any type can be drawn as a lane or pool), a shape in it is **assigned** to it instead of getting it as its parent: a task in the role lane *Approver* performs the role *Approver*, a task in the role lane *Approver* of the pool *Purchasing Department* has both connections. A new element gets as parent the nearest container that is no such lane, and you are not asked *New parent?* for the lane itself. Moving the shape to another lane removes the old connection and adds the new one; moving it out of every lane, or removing it from the diagram, removes the connection and needs `entity:delete`. An assignment that a lane stands for has no *×*. Shapes that already lie in a lane when you change the viewpoint or the organization model get their connection when you move them. A lane of another type, for example *Lane*, stays the parent as before.

Assignments are ordinary connections: a [catalog](./catalogs) of lanes can join *Belongs To* and filter by the unit, and a catalog of user tasks can join *Responsible* to show the person; a task without an assignment shows an empty cell.

## Details of an entity

Select a shape to open the details panel of its entity beside the canvas. It shows the name, the description, the type and the version of the entity and one field for each property of its type, in the order of the type; a required property is marked. Close the panel with the close button or with Escape; Tab leads from the selected shape into the panel.

- **Read:** every holder of `entity:read` sees the values. A value you may not read, for example one that is restricted to a role, shows as *Restricted*. Without `entity:write` the panel is read-only.
- **Change:** with `entity:write` you change the name, the description and the values of the properties you may write. A property that is restricted to a role you do not hold shows its value without an input field. For a reference property you search the entities of the referenced type by name. An empty input removes the value.
- **Complete an incomplete entity:** the panel names the missing required values (*Required values missing: …*). Enter them and choose *Save*; the shape then loses its *Incomplete:* mark. A required property that you may not change stays required, the entity stays incomplete until someone who may writes it.
- **Save:** your changes in the details count as unsaved changes of the diagram, with the *Unsaved changes* mark, and are saved together with the layout by *Save*, in one step: either everything is saved or nothing. A save changes at most 500 entities; the canvas tells when the limit is reached. Changing an entity that is still incomplete requires its required values, so complete it in the same step.
- **A new entity:** the details of a new shape take a description and values as well; they are created with the entity.

If someone else changed an entity since you opened its details, *Save* answers *… was changed by someone else* naming the entity, and saves nothing. Your changes stay on the canvas; choose *Reload … and discard my changes of it* to read the entity again (this discards only your changes of that entity), then repeat your change and save again. An entity with open issues of a solution cannot be changed; the message names the entity and its issues, and your changes stay. A value the server rejects is marked at its field with the reason.

## Connections

A connection is an entity of its own: it joins a source entity with a target entity and has a type, a name and values. A diagram shows a connection as an **edge**, a straight line between two shapes with an arrow at the target, the name of the connection and, if you add them, bend points. The line runs from a shape of the connection's source to a shape of its target, so the direction follows the connection. **An edge is a view of a connection: the connection itself stays in the repository.** The same connection may have several edges in one diagram, and not every connection between two shapes needs to be shown.

- **Draw a connection:** with `entity:write`, drag from a handle at the left or right side of a shape to another shape. OEA offers the connection types that the viewpoint and the metamodel allow between the two types. With one type, it is used; with several, you choose one; with none, the canvas says *No connection is allowed here.* A type that is allowed only from the second shape to the first is drawn from the second shape. The connection is created when you choose *Save*, in the same step as the layout, and it is named after its ends (*Source → Target*). If its type has a mandatory property without a default value, the connection is still created and counts as **incomplete** until the value is written; the edge shows *Incomplete:* with the names. A connection of a type that controls the visibility of properties (a *scoping connection type*) needs the permission `visibility-scope:assign` in addition; without it, *Save* answers that you may not create connections of this type.
- **Show existing connections:** a diagram does not show the existing connections of an entity by itself. When you place an existing entity with *Add existing* and it has connections to entities that are already on the canvas, a notice says *N connections available*. Choose *Show all* to add their edges, *Choose…* to pick some of them, or *Dismiss*; nothing is added without your choice. Only connections of types the viewpoint allows are offered, and deleted connections are not.
- **Add a connected element:** select a shape and choose *Add connected element* (also in the context menu of the shape). Choose the connection type and the type of the new element among those that the viewpoint and the metamodel allow from the selected type. The new shape is placed to the right of the selected one, at the default length of the connection type: the distance between the two borders, 90 pt without a stored length (see [Viewpoints](../administration/metamodel-editing#viewpoints)). Name the new shape as usual.
- **Bend points:** double-click an edge to add a bend point, drag a bend point of the selected edge to move it, and double-click a bend point to remove it. An edge has at most 50 bend points.
- **Select and remove:** reach an edge with Tab or click it; its details open next to the canvas (see [Details of an entity](#details-of-an-entity)). *Remove from diagram*, Delete or the context menu of the edge removes only the edge; **the connection stays in the repository.** Removing a shape removes its edges, and a connection that was only drawn and not yet saved disappears with its last edge.
- **Limits:** a diagram holds at most 4000 edges, and one save creates at most 500 new connections; the canvas tells when a limit is reached.

If a connection is deleted, its edges are hidden; they show again when it is restored. A stored edge stays when the viewpoint no longer allows its connection type, but it is a conflict (see [Change the viewpoint](#change-the-viewpoint)), and you cannot draw new ones of that type.

## Change the viewpoint {#change-the-viewpoint}

With `entity:write`, *Change viewpoint* above the canvas changes the viewpoint of the diagram. The button is disabled while you have unsaved changes: save or discard them first. The dialog lists the viewpoints; when you choose one, it names before the change the shapes and edges whose type that viewpoint does not allow (*2 shapes become conflicts.*, or *No conflicts arise.*). The change is saved at once, as a change of its own; the shapes, edges and lines stay as they are, and the canvas reloads with the palette and the default sizes of the new viewpoint. If someone else changed the diagram meanwhile, the dialog says so and offers to reload.

**Conflicts.** A shape or edge whose type the diagram's viewpoint does not allow is a *conflict*. This happens after a change of the viewpoint, and also when an administrator removes the type from the viewpoint or a new version of a [metamodel package](../administration/metamodel-packages) does. OEA checks the exact type: a viewpoint that allows a type does not allow its subtypes. Conflicts are marked by a dashed border (or line) and the text *Conflict: type not allowed in this viewpoint*, not only by color. The list *Conflicts (N)* above the canvas names them; choosing a name selects the shape or edge. Shapes of a deleted entity are hidden and are no conflicts, but they can become conflicts when the entity is restored.

**Resolve them.** *Remove from diagram* in the list removes one conflict with its edges and hierarchy lines (for a container with nested shapes, OEA asks first, because the nested shapes leave the diagram with it), and *Remove all conflicts* removes all. The entities stay in the repository. Or an administrator allows the type again in the viewpoint; the mark then disappears. **As long as a conflict is left, you cannot save the diagram:** *Save* answers *The diagram was not saved: it still has conflicts*, whatever else you changed, and keeps your changes. A reader sees the marks and the list without the buttons.

## Rename and delete a diagram

*Rename* changes the name and the description of a diagram, on the page *Diagrams* and on the page of the diagram. If someone else changed the diagram meanwhile, the dialog says so; reload the page and repeat your change.

*Delete* asks for a confirmation. A deleted diagram no longer appears in the list, the browser and the navigation tree, and its name can be used again. **The entities of the repository stay.** The diagram itself can be restored (see below).

## Restore a diagram

With the permission `entity:restore`, the switch *Show deleted* on the page *Diagrams* lists the deleted diagrams with the time and the person of the deletion. Choose *Restore* to bring a diagram back, together with its entries in the navigation tree. If another diagram has taken its name meanwhile, the page says so and the diagram stays deleted; rename the other diagram first.

## Deleting a viewpoint

Diagrams cannot exist without their viewpoint. When you delete a viewpoint that diagrams use, they are deleted with it, **for good** and including the deleted ones; the entities stay. The confirmation names the number of diagrams that will be deleted (see [Viewpoints](../administration/metamodel-editing#viewpoints)). The same happens when a new version of a [metamodel package](../administration/metamodel-packages) no longer carries a system-defined viewpoint.

## Through the API

The diagrams are available through `/diagrams` (see the [API reference](../api/)):

- `GET /diagrams` lists the diagrams by name. `q` is a part of the name (at most 200 characters), `pageSize` is 1 to 200 (default 50), and `page` starts at 0. The answer carries `nextCursor`; send it as `cursor` together with `page` increased by 1 to continue. A cursor of another query is rejected with 400 naming `cursor`.
- `POST /diagrams` creates a diagram from `name`, `description` and `viewpointId`; `GET /diagrams/{id}` reads it; `PUT /diagrams/{id}` changes `name` and `description` (a `viewpointId` is ignored; `PUT /diagrams/{id}/viewpoint` changes the viewpoint, see below); `DELETE /diagrams/{id}` deletes it. Every change needs the header `If-Match` with the version that was read (`"3"`); without it the answer is 428, with another version 412. The answers carry the new version as `ETag`.
- `GET /diagrams/deleted` lists the deleted diagrams, and `POST /diagrams/{id}/restore` with `If-Match` restores one; both need `entity:restore`. A diagram that is not deleted answers 409 (`urn:oea:problem:diagram-not-deleted`).
- A taken name answers 422 with the problem type `urn:oea:problem:diagram-name-taken`; other invalid input answers 422 and names the field in `invalidFields` (`name`, `description` or `viewpointId`). An unknown or deleted diagram answers 404.
- Every change is recorded in the audit trail as `diagram.created`, `diagram.updated`, `diagram.deleted` or `diagram.restored`.
- `PUT /diagrams/{id}/viewpoint` with `If-Match` and `entity:write` changes the viewpoint to the one in `viewpointId`; it is saved at once and the answer is the diagram with `conflictCount`, the number of conflicts. Shapes, edges and lines stay. An unknown viewpoint answers 422 naming `viewpointId`; the viewpoint the diagram already has changes nothing (no new version, no audit). The change is recorded as `diagram.viewpoint-changed` with `fromViewpointId`, `toViewpointId` and `conflicts`.
- `GET /views/diagrams/{id}` marks every shape and edge with `conflict` and carries `conflictCount`. `PUT /views/diagrams/{id}` answers 409 with the problem type `urn:oea:problem:diagram-conflicts` and `invalidFields` such as `shapes[3]` and `edges[1]` while the saved layout still contains a shape or edge that is a conflict, whatever else the save changes (also a save without any change); nothing is changed then. A layout error (422) is answered first, the missing permission to write a property after the conflicts. A save that removes all conflicts is accepted; a new shape or edge of a type the viewpoint does not allow is still rejected with 422 `METATYPE_NOT_ALLOWED`.
- `GET /views/diagrams/{id}` returns the view of a diagram: the diagram with its version (`ETag`), its viewpoint with the effective default sizes, and the shapes in drawing order (the last is drawn on top) with the shape ID, the entity, the position and the size in pt, and `missingMandatory`, the names of the mandatory properties without a value. Shapes of a deleted entity are left out.
- `PUT /views/diagrams/{id}` saves the layout with `If-Match`: all `shapes` in drawing order (`id`, exactly one of `entityId` and `newEntityKey`, `x`, `y`, `width`, `height`) and the `newEntities` (`key`, `metatypeId`, `name`) that the shapes with `newEntityKey` refer to. The save is atomic and raises the version. New entities are created like entities of the batch write (`entity.created`), but an entity with a missing mandatory property is created incomplete instead of being rejected. Limits: 2000 shapes, 500 new entities, positions within ±100 000 pt, sizes 1 to 10 000 pt, at most two decimals, a body of 4 MB; violations answer 400 or 413. A faulty shape or new entity answers 422 with `invalidFields` and `errors` (for example `shapes[3].entityId` or `newEntities[1].name`); nothing is saved then. A shape ID of another diagram is rejected too.
- `GET /views/diagrams/{id}/entities` searches the entities that the viewpoint allows (`q` up to 255 characters, `metatypeId`, `page`, `pageSize` 1 to 200); a metatype the viewpoint does not allow answers 422 naming `metatypeId`.
- Every saved layout is recorded in the audit trail as `diagram.layout-saved` with the numbers of added, changed and removed shapes and edges, of created entities and connections and of entities whose parent the save changed (`parentsChanged`).
- `GET /views/diagrams/{id}/entities/{entityId}` returns the details of an entity that a shape of the diagram shows: `name`, `description`, `metatype`, `version`, the values in `properties` (a value you may not read has `redacted: true`), in `fields` for each mapped property whether you may read (`readable`) and change (`writable`) it, and `missingMandatory`. An entity without a shape in the diagram, a deleted entity and a deleted diagram answer 404. The data types, enumeration values and limits of the properties come from the metatype (`GET /metamodel/metatypes/{id}`).
- `GET /views/diagrams/{id}/reference-targets?propertyDefId=…` lists the entities a reference property may point to: the entities of exactly the referenced type, by name (`q` up to 255 characters, `page`, `pageSize` 1 to 200). A property that is not a reference property answers 422 naming `propertyDefId`.
- `PUT /views/diagrams/{id}` also carries `entityUpdates`, at most 500: each names an `entityId` that a shape of the request shows (once), the `expectedVersion` read (at least 1) and optionally `name` (not blank, up to 255 characters), `description` (up to 2000) and `properties` (`propertyDefId` and `value`; an empty `value` removes the value). Fields and properties that are not named keep their value. The changes are written in the same transaction as the layout, with the checks and the audit of a batch update (`entity.updated`); a changed entity needs its mandatory values (422 `MANDATORY_MISSING` on `entityUpdates[i].properties`), and a restricted property without write access answers 403 for the whole save. A `newEntities` item can carry `description` and `properties` as well. An entity with another current version answers 409 with the problem type `urn:oea:problem:entity-version-conflict` and `conflicts` (`entityId` and `currentVersion` of every stale entity); an entity with an open issue of a solution answers 409 `urn:oea:problem:entity-blocked`. Nothing is changed then. The batch API (`POST /entities/batch`) keeps writing without a version.
- `GET /views/diagrams/{id}` also returns the `edges`: each with the edge ID, the `connection` (`id`, `name`, `metatypeId`), the `sourceShapeId` and `targetShapeId`, the bend `points` in pt and `missingMandatory`. Edges of a deleted connection or of a shape of a deleted entity are left out.
- `PUT /views/diagrams/{id}` also carries `edges` (at most 4000; the saved set replaces the stored one) and `newConnections` (at most 500). An edge has an ID, exactly one of `connectionId` and `newConnectionKey`, the `sourceShapeId` and `targetShapeId` of shapes of the same request, and `points` (at most 50, each within ±100 000 pt with at most two decimals). The source shape must show the source entity of the connection and the target shape its target entity; a new edge needs a connection type that the viewpoint allows. A `newConnections` item has a `key`, a `metatypeId` and optionally `name`, `description` and `properties`; its ends are the entities of the shapes of its first edge, and it needs at least one edge. New connections are created after the new entities with the checks and the audit of a connection of the batch write (`entity.created`; the codes `SOURCE_NOT_ALLOWED` and `TARGET_NOT_ALLOWED` name `newConnections[i]`), a missing mandatory value does not block them, and a connection of a scoping type needs `visibility-scope:assign` (403 for the whole save, before the version check). A faulty edge or new connection answers 422 naming `edges[i]` or `newConnections[i]`; more than 4000 edges, 50 bend points on an edge or 500 new connections answer 400. `entityUpdates` may name a connection that an edge of the request shows. The body is limited to 4 MB. `diagram.layout-saved` also carries `edgesAdded`, `edgesChanged`, `edgesRemoved` and `connectionsCreated`.
- `GET /views/diagrams/{id}/entities/{entityId}` also answers for a connection that an edge of the diagram shows; a connection without an edge, or with a hidden edge, answers 404.
- `GET /entities/{id}/deletion` previews "Delete from repository" (`entity:delete`): the entity with its version, the number of its children and of the connections deleted with them, and the active solutions with the number of their changes. `DELETE /entities/{id}` with `If-Match` deletes the entity, its children on all levels and their connections with one time and actor and answers the IDs of everything deleted; a connection is deleted alone. Open issues answer 409 `entity-blocked`, a structuring aid or an automatic composition connection 422, a connection of a scoping type without `visibility-scope:assign` 403. `POST /entities/{id}/undelete` with `If-Match` restores the entity with exactly its cascade (`entity:restore`) and names the connections that stay deleted because their other endpoint is still deleted; an entity that is not deleted answers 409 `entity-not-deleted`. The events are `entity.deleted`, `entity.cascade-deleted` and `entity.undeleted`.
- `POST /views/diagrams/{id}/connections/query` with `entityIds` (1 to 2000 entities just placed) and `shownEntityIds` (0 to 2000 other shown entities) returns the connections of allowed types that are not deleted and join a placed entity with a placed or shown one, by ID, at most 5000, with `truncated` for more (`entity:read`; it only reads and is not audited).
- `GET /views/diagrams/{id}` also returns `parentShapeId` on each shape (the container; `null` for a shape on the canvas, and also when the container is hidden, comes later in the drawing order or does not contain the shape any more), `childCount` and `parentEntityId` on its entity, the `hierarchyLines` (`id`, `parentShapeId`, `childShapeId`; only those whose hierarchy holds) and the effective setting `askNewParent`.
- `PUT /views/diagrams/{id}` also carries `parentShapeId` per shape (a shape of the same request earlier in the list, at most 10 levels deep, without a cycle, that contains the shape completely; positions stay absolute), `hierarchyLines` (at most 4000, each pair of shapes once, and the entity of the child shape must be a child of the entity of the container shape after the parent changes of the same save) and `parentChanges` (at most 500; each names an `entityId` that a shape of the request shows, once, the `expectedVersion` and at most one of `parentEntityId` and `parentNewEntityKey`; neither clears the parent). A `newEntities` item can carry `parentEntityId` or `parentNewEntityKey` and then ends at version 2. The parents are written in the transaction of the save with the rules, the automatic composition connection and the audit `entity.parent-changed` of `PUT /entities/{id}/parent`; a change to the current parent writes nothing. A stale version answers 409 `entity-version-conflict` (`invalidFields` names `parentChanges[i]`), an open issue 409 `entity-blocked`. A faulty container, line or parent answers 422 naming `shapes[i].parentShapeId`, `hierarchyLines[i]`, `parentChanges[i]` or `newEntities[i].parentEntityId`; more than 4000 lines or 500 parent changes answer 400.
- `GET /views/diagrams/{id}/entities/{entityId}/children` lists the direct children of an entity that are not deleted by name (at most 500, `truncated` for more), each with `allowed` (the viewpoint allows its type) and its composition connection (`compositionConnection`, with `allowed` for its type). An unknown, deleted or connection entity answers 404 (`entity:read`; it only reads and is not audited).
